---
name: Recruiting industry rules
description: Product intent and safety constraints for Recruiting & Staffing.
---

Recruiting & Staffing sells the user's actual confirmed recruiting services to hiring employers, not to candidates. Employer sectors must remain distinct from role families: a software role at a manufacturer does not turn the employer into a Technology company.

**Why:** The user explicitly requested employer-centric discovery, hiring research, seller-service matching, outreach and hiring-needs discovery calls using the existing IT workflow as the reference.

**How to apply:** Distinguish cited hiring evidence from inferred workforce needs, commercial service fit from evidence confidence, and a likely HR/operations stakeholder from a verified decision maker. A job ad is not willingness to use an agency; formation or relocation alone is not confirmed hiring. Unknown posting age must stay unknown; an old listing is not automatically hard-to-fill.

The approved recruiting workflow is jobs first, then job-level hiring filters, employer research and company-level filters, and contact lookup only for qualified employers. Other seller industries must retain their existing workflow.

**Why:** The user explicitly approved replacing company-first discovery after seeing businesses with contacts but no verified openings. This supersedes their earlier proposal to discover companies through Apollo first.

**How to apply:** City/state describe job-search geography, not employer headquarters. Company size, independently verified industry and service fit require employer research after job filtering. AI must interpret cited sources, not substitute for them. Growth signals supplement job-found employers; do not imply direct integrations or exhaustive job-board access. Do not spend contact credits on unverified or agency-restricted employers.

Employers and preliminary postings found through job discovery should remain explicitly reviewable when subsequent employer research is unavailable; this does not qualify them for recruiting outreach.

**Why:** The user expects to review employers with service-match filtering disabled. A research failure must not be presented as evidence that no businesses exist.

**How to apply:** Offer discovery-only review without automatic contact-credit spending, preserving preliminary posting titles, locations and sources as unverified. Evidence-dependent filters must still exclude unknown matches. Keep research failures distinct from genuine empty results, and never fall back silently to business-directory results.

Recruiting search verification must cover both approved and Needs Review seller profiles.

**Why:** A ready QA seller can receive service matches while an unreviewed tenant receives none for the same location. A successful QA search alone does not validate the user's approval-dependent path.

**How to apply:** Warn before confirmed-service searches with an unreviewed profile, never auto-approve services, and provide explicit discovery-only review of already returned businesses without another provider search or automatic contact lookup. Preserve review-only labels when restoring a saved search. A provider-only discovery check is not end-to-end proof; verify authenticated qualification and contact gating separately.

Industry switching must not import another industry's approved seller claims or destroy those approvals when switching back.

**Why:** A company can sell different services across verticals. Reusing the previous ready profile after a failed new-industry analysis creates confident but incorrect outreach; discarding it loses the user's confirmed edits.

**How to apply:** Keep selected-industry seller context independent, retain prior per-industry approvals, and fall back to discovery-only messaging when that industry's seller evidence is unavailable. Service priorities and differentiator inputs must switch with the profile, not remain account-global; unchanged full-form text is not approval to import another industry's catalog.

Recruiters should be able to discover hiring locations rather than know them before searching.

**Why:** The user said a recruiter is looking for any location an employer is hiring for, and questioned requiring a hiring location before the research has found it.

**How to apply:** Treat extra hiring-location detail as an optional narrowing preference, not a prerequisite for employer research. Explain that the job-search area is independent of employer headquarters and other hiring sites, and disclose geographic discovery limits rather than promising exhaustive nationwide coverage.

Explicit no-agencies/no-unsolicited-resumes evidence blocks automated recruiting pitches. This currently uses conservative employer-wide suppression pending source review, including an employer careers-page policy without any active job ads.

**Why:** The user explicitly prohibited pitching affected opportunities; a general employer policy can apply beyond the particular advertised role. Avoid circumventing a policy through another channel or a different campaign.

**How to apply:** Preserve the policy citation, show why outreach is blocked, and re-evaluate it during research refresh. Never invent candidates, placement results, or delivery guarantees.
